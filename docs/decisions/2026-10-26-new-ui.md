# A new UI for the page (2026-10-26)

A proposal for the owner to decide on, not a build: `src/ShotDetectorWeb.App` is unchanged by it. The page's first
design is [2026-10-24-the-page.md](2026-10-24-the-page.md) ("The page itself"), its structure
[2026-10-25-one-page.md](2026-10-25-one-page.md) (one timeline, cards, a module per panel). Since then seven panels
have landed and five more are on their way: a lane of the scores (#36) and a card of warnings (#39), Stop and Pause
(#42, the pause row), progress per range when a long video runs across workers
([2026-10-26-workers.md](2026-10-26-workers.md)), and a still per shot (backlog "Thumbnails per shot"). The question is
what the page should look like with all of them on it. A later session builds the direction chosen here, once the
panel sessions running now have landed, as the backlog row "The page's new UI".

What was looked at: the live page (`main` at `31f354e`) as a visitor sees it, "Try the demo" run in headless Chromium
at 1280 px and 390 px wide (2026-10-26, Playwright, the AOT bundle Pages serves), and the three open pull requests'
panels. A static mockup of the proposed layout, with that run's numbers typed in and the lanes drawn by hand, is
[../mockups/2026-10-26-new-ui.html](../mockups/2026-10-26-new-ui.html) (open it as a file; `?state=idle`, `detecting`
or `done`). Screenshots of today's page and the mockup are in the pull request that added this file.

## What a visitor sees today

The demo on the trailer: 21.1 s, 59 frames per second, 8 shots. Then, on a 1280 px window:

1. **The result starts below the fold.** The pitch, the drop zone, the demo's paragraph and the options keep their
   full size after a file is picked, so the video starts 543 px down (847 px at 390 px wide), after a status line.
   The page is 2,836 px tall (3,715 on the phone), of which the run's result, the showcase's point, is one card of
   113 px, second in the right column.
2. **The timeline is narrow and the card column is tall.** The video takes three fifths of the width and the timeline
   its width, about 540 px of track for 52 s once the lane labels are off. Beside them the cards stack to about
   1,750 px: bitrate (303 px), the run (113), shot statistics (342), sound (551), about this file (301). The left
   column ends at 1,050 px, so under the timeline is 700 px of nothing. The scores lane (#36, 4.5rem) and a ranges
   lane will make the timeline taller, not wider.
3. **The cards' order is the scripts' order**, and bitrate is first because it shows on `opened` before anything else
   does. Shown first on a finished run is "1,253 video packets, 9.20 MB, 17 keyframes", and "8 shots in 1253 frames
   at 24 fps, 52.21 s" with the downloads under it. Nothing on the page says what the downloads are: that they are
   scenedetect's CSVs byte for byte is in the header's sentence and nowhere near them.
4. **Three ways of saying whose number it is.** The sound card has "measured" chips on its headings, the statistics
   card has a "the page's own" chip on its title, and the bitrate card underlines whole sentences dotted ("1.41 Mbit/s
   on average, 4.24 Mbit/s at most in one second"), which reads as broken links, with a note at the bottom saying what
   the underline means. The sound card uses the dotted underline too, for a silence's length.
5. **The shot playing is far from the player.** "Now: shot 1 of 8, 0.0 s into 13.9 s" is a line in the statistics card,
   third in the right column, 1,250 px from the video; the table marks the row below.
6. **The status line is long**: "Detecting: frame 211 of 1253, 53 frames per second. Estimate: about 27 s for 1253 frames
   at 854x480 (46 frames per second, from a reference run of Sintel's trailer)." wraps to two lines, and the progress bar
   sits between the form and the video.
7. **At 390 px** everything stacks in script order: video, timeline (its lanes 290 px wide), six cards, the table, so a
   visitor scrolls past 2,300 px of cards to the shots.

None of this is a bug; each row put its panel where the structure says. It is what ten panels on the first release's
layout add up to.

## Keep how the page is built?

### Question

The page is `index.html`, `style.css`, `app.js`, `timeline.js` and a module per panel: no framework, no build step, no
dependency, a visitor reads the source as it is (decision "The page itself"). Does a new UI need more?

### Choices

1. **Keep it.** The layout is the skeleton in `index.html`, `style.css`'s grid and the one place `card()` puts a card.
   The panels never say where they are: a lane is `lane(label, draw)`, a card is `card(title)`, both from the page,
   and the panels' data flow (the events, `call`) doesn't change. A new UI is a new skeleton and stylesheet, and a
   `card()` that takes a place.
2. **A component framework** (React, Lit, Svelte): a build step, a dependency, source a visitor can't read as it is,
   and nothing the layout below needs. The owner declined Blazor and a build step on 2026-10-24.
3. **Web Components without a build step**: custom elements for lane and card. The same thing as 1 with more
   ceremony; the panels already are modules.

### Answer

**1, proposed.** The one-page decision made the layout a thing the panels don't know about; that is what makes a new
UI a change to three files and a word in each panel rather than a rewrite. The whole page is about 1,900 lines of
JavaScript and CSS; the proposal below adds less than it moves.

## The layout

### Question

Where does everything go, with the five coming panels on the page?

### Choices

**A. Tidy the layout there is.** Fold the landing into a line once a file is picked; order the cards by what a
visitor wants first (the run, then the statistics, the sound, the file, the bitrate), with `card(title, { order })`
or by reordering the script lines; one provenance mark; the table gets a thumbnail column. A day's work, and the
result moves up by about 300 px. The timeline stays 540 px wide beside a column of cards twice its height, and every
lane to come makes the imbalance worse; the shot playing stays in a card.

**B. The timeline across the page; the video and the run side by side; the details last.** The page as a workbench
whose spine is the time axis. State by state:

- *Idle*: the landing, one screen: the pitch (two sentences), the drop zone with "Try the demo" inside it and the
  trailer's credit beside the button, the options and the Detect button under it. Nothing else until a file is
  picked.
- *A file picked* (opening, detecting, done, stopped, error): the landing folds into a **toolbar**: the title, the file
  (name, size, and once opened its size, rate, length and audio in a line: the description's first facts), "Change",
  the three options, and one button at the right: Detect shots, then Pause and Stop while a run goes, Cancel while
  opening (#42's states). A **status line** under the toolbar, one line: a thin progress bar and "Frame 412 of 1,253 ·
  52 frames per second · about 16 s left" while detecting (the estimate's provenance in its tooltip, not the line);
  "Done in 21.1 s, 59 frames per second (2.5 times real time)" after; an error in the same place, in the library's
  words as today.
- *The result*, a grid of four rows:
  1. **The player and the run.** The video on the left, three fifths of the width as now, with the shot playing as a
     caption under it ("Shot 2 of 8 · 00:00:13.917 – 00:00:16.500 · 1.2 s in"; "3 shots so far" while a run goes).
     Beside it, the **run's card**: the figures large (shots, cuts, length, frames and rate, the detector and threshold
     the run used), the two downloads with one line saying what they are ("what `scenedetect -s` writes") and the mark
     "scenedetect's, byte for byte", the warnings (#39) under them when there are any, and a stopped run's "Stopped at
     00:00:25.333 (frame 608 of 1253)" (#42) where the figures are.
  2. **The timeline across the whole width**: 1,150 px of track at 1280 px instead of 540; the lanes as they are
     (cuts, scores, bitrate, sound, measures when there is something to mark) with room for a ranges lane during a
     split run (one bar per worker, filling as it goes: the workers decision's "progress per range"), under the
     cuts. `timeline.js` doesn't change; it is in a wider box.
  3. **The shots**, in their scroll box as today, with a **still per shot** as the first column (the browser's decode,
     marked as such), lazily as rows come into view (the thumbnails row). At phone width the End column goes (it is
     the next row's start) and the stills shrink.
  4. **The details**: shot statistics, sound, bitrate and keyframes, about this file, as cards in a grid of equal
     columns (`repeat(auto-fill, minmax(17rem, 1fr))`: four at 1280 px, two at 800, one on a phone), each a dl of its
     numbers with the long notes gone into tooltips. Bitrate's "5 of 7 cuts fall on a keyframe" moves to the statistics
     card as one row (a reading about the shots), or stays; the owner's call.
- *Phone*: the same rows, one column: toolbar, status, video, run, timeline, shots, details.
- *Width*: the page's `max-width` from 64rem to 80rem. The timeline and the table are what the width is for; prose is
  in cards of 17rem or more, so lines don't get long.

What it costs: `index.html`'s skeleton (the toolbar and the four grid areas), `style.css` (most of it), `app.js`'s
`card(title, { place })` with `run`, `details` (the default) and `below` (the shots), the toolbar's folding (a class on
`body` from `choose()`), the status line's words, the caption under the video (the "Now" line moves out of
`panels/stats.js`, which keeps the figures), and the thumbnails row's column. Each panel changes by a word or nothing:
`run.js` and `warnings.js` say `place: 'run'`; `shots.js` already says `below`. The lanes don't change at all. A
visitor at 1280 px sees the video, the run and the whole timeline in the first screen; the mockup's done state is
2,188 px tall against 2,836 today, with the stills and the warnings in it.

**C. An application: a sidebar and an inspector.** The file and the options in a left sidebar, the video and the
timeline in the middle, a right inspector with tabs (Run, Shots, Sound, File). It looks like a tool, and it hides
everything but one tab, which undoes the one-page decision's point: a cut, a loud moment and a bitrate spike line up
by eye only when they are all on screen. Also the most CSS and the most state of the three.

### Answer

**B, proposed**; the owner decides. A keeps the problem (the timeline's width and the column's height) and C
undoes the last decision. B is the one-page decision taken to its end: the timeline is the page, and what isn't
over time is around it in the order a visitor wants it.

## One mark for whose number it is

### Question

The page shows three kinds of number: the CSVs, checked byte for byte; the core's measures, checked against FFmpeg
within a tolerance; and what the page works out for display. Today each card says so its own way (finding 4). One way?

### Choices

1. **One chip, three words, once per card**, on the card's title: "scenedetect's, byte for byte" (the run), "measured"
   (sound), "the core's" (about this file), "the page's" (statistics, bitrate), each with the same tooltip shape ("The
   core's measure, checked against FFmpeg's ebur128 within 0.1 LU"). A line in the footer says what the three words
   mean. No dotted underlines; a card that mixes kinds (sound's silences' lengths) says so in that row's tooltip.
2. **As today**, each panel its own convention.

### Answer

**1, proposed.** The marks are the page's honesty about exactness, which is what the showcase claims; they should
look like one thing. The sound card's "measured" chips are already that chip.

## Not in this proposal

- **A charting library, a framework, a build step**: decided, above.
- **Thumbnails from ManagedCodecs**: the browser's decode, as "Pictures of shots" decided.
- **Re-thresholding on the page**: a new threshold is a new run (the scores row's Not).
- **A dark theme of its own**: the page follows the system as now; the mockup uses the same tokens.
- **Changing what any panel computes or says**: the proposal moves panels and marks them; their numbers, words and
  checks stay.
